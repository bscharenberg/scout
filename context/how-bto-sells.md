# How BTO sells

This is how BTO Group wins work, in plain language. The sales assistant reads it before every conversation. If you adapt this demo for a real firm, this is the first file to rewrite.

## The motion

BTO sells through relationships and referrals. Most new clients come through partner networks, people who used to work at client firms, and private equity sponsors who have hired BTO before. BTO doesn't win RFPs it didn't help shape. When an RFP arrives cold, the first question is whether BTO can get a real conversation with the buyer before the deadline. If it can't, the pursuit is usually not worth the cost.

The person who sells the work is a senior person who will stay on it. A typical engagement team is three to six people, led by a partner or principal who works on the engagement at least two days a week. The buyer meets the delivery lead before the proposal goes out. This is the main reason BTO wins against larger firms, so reps should make it visible early, not save it for the price conversation.

## Deal stages

Each stage has a clear test for moving on. If the test isn't met, the deal hasn't moved, whatever the CRM says.

1. **Introduction.** A warm introduction or referral leads to a first meeting. Move on when there is a named person who owns a real problem and has agreed to a second conversation.
2. **Discovery.** BTO learns the problem in the client's own words, why it matters now, and who else has a say. Move on when the rep can write the problem in two sentences the client would agree with, knows who signs, and knows what happens if the client does nothing.
3. **Point of view.** BTO shares a short, honest view of what it thinks is going on and what it would do first, usually in a working session rather than a deck. This is where the Ground Truth idea shows up. Move on when the champion agrees the view is right and the economic buyer has heard it, from BTO or from the champion.
4. **Proposal.** BTO writes a proposal and walks through it live with the champion and, ideally, the economic buyer. BTO doesn't email a proposal and wait. Move on when the decision process, decision date, and any competing options are known.
5. **Commercials and paper.** The SOW, the master agreement, and procurement. Move on when it's signed.
6. **Handoff to delivery.** The delivery lead was already in the sales process, so this is a short step: confirm access to people and data, and set the first three weeks.

## Healthy and unhealthy signs

Healthy: the rep has met more than one person, the economic buyer has heard BTO's view, the client has shared something they wouldn't share with a vendor, and the next meeting is on the calendar.

Unhealthy: only one contact (single-threaded), the economic buyer hasn't been met or was skeptical and nobody addressed it, the proposal was emailed and not walked through, a stakeholder who was never in the room has started to have opinions, or silence of more than a week after a proposal.

When a deal goes quiet after a proposal, BTO's habit is to send one short, direct note that makes it easy for the champion to say what changed. Not "just checking in". If that gets no answer, the rep tries to reach the economic buyer once, then marks the deal as stalled in the forecast rather than hoping.

## Pricing in the sale

Fixed fee is the default. If a buyer needs a lower number, BTO reduces the scope rather than discounting the fee. A short diagnostic sprint (see the offerings file) is the usual way to lower the first commitment. Reps don't talk about price before discovery is done.
