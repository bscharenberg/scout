# Shaping a deal into a proposal and SOW

## Proposal structure

1. **Situation.** The client's problem in their own words. If they feel understood, they'll read the rest.
2. **What we heard.** The main themes from discovery. It shows BTO listened.
3. **Our point of view.** What BTO believes is true, including anything uncomfortable. This is what sets BTO apart.
4. **Approach.** Phases, workstreams, and milestones. Three phases at most for a first engagement.
5. **Team.** Name the people and their relevant experience. No "supported by our bench of experts".
6. **Investment.** A fixed fee with payment milestones. Optionally a smaller first phase, such as a diagnostic sprint.
7. **Why now.** One honest paragraph on what waiting costs. No fear tactics.

## What goes in the SOW

- **Scope:** what's included and, just as important, what's excluded.
- **Deliverables:** named outputs with formats, for example "an operating model blueprint of 25 to 35 pages with a decision rights matrix".
- **Milestones and payments:** tied to finished deliverables, not calendar dates.
- **Access:** the people, time, and data BTO needs from the client. Unclear access is the most common cause of delays.
- **Change process:** how scope changes are raised, priced, and approved. Agree it in the SOW, before the first change request.
- **IP and confidentiality:** BTO keeps its methods. The client owns the deliverables made for them.

## Friction to expect, by buyer type

**Enterprise with in-house legal:** expect their master agreement and SOW template. Push to use BTO's SOW for scope clarity and accept their master agreement if needed. Watch for unlimited liability, broad IP assignment, wide non-solicitation clauses, and payment terms of 90 days or more. BTO's standard position: liability capped at fees paid in the prior six months, IP assignment limited to client-specific deliverables, payment in 30 days.

**Mid-market with lean legal or outside counsel:** often willing to use BTO's standard agreement. Watch for personal guarantee requests from PE-backed companies, change-of-control clauses, and vague scope that invites creep.

**PE-backed portfolio companies:** the fund may need to approve vendors, so find out early. Sponsor IP language is often aggressive. Hold the line on BTO's methods. Payment risk is real in distressed situations, so use more milestone payments.

## Pricing principles

- Fixed fee is the default. Time and materials invites scope arguments and misaligned incentives.
- A diagnostic sprint of $75K to $120K is a good first step for a cautious buyer.
- Don't discount the fee. Adjust the scope. Discounting teaches clients to expect it.
- Tying 5 to 15 percent of the fee to defined outcomes can work for the right relationship, but only with very precise outcome definitions in the SOW. Don't offer it casually.
